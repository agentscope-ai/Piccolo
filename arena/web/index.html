Ranking & stability methods
  1. Comparisons use the registered game, seed and opponent policy. Equal final scores draw. A decision-budget limit preserves the last actual score.
  2. Per-game Elo starts at 1500 with K = 32. A win, draw or loss scores 1, 0.5 or 0; errors are unscored. Overall Elo averages all games equally and requires full coverage. The rule engine enters supported game rankings only.
  3. Adding a model schedules its new pairings only. Completed baseline matches and matching single-player episodes are reused. Ratings with few games are provisional.
  4. Turn-based decisions have three independent requests with shuffled choices. Realtime games use one request and have no three-probe stability metrics. Votes are aligned by action; complete mean probabilities break ties. Agreement is the largest vote share.
  5. Probability metrics require three complete distributions. Variance uses sample variance. Jensen–Shannon distance uses base 2 and averages all three pairs; probability coverage remains available in the record details.
  6. Probability stability is defined here as 1 − mean Jensen–Shannon distance. Stability does not measure correctness or affect Elo. Rule-engine decisions have no model stability metrics.
